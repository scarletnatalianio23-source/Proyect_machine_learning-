Conclusion

{{ model_names[best] }} is the most accurate of the three, with an average error of {{ m[best].mae }} ha, {{ ((m.linear.mae - m[best].mae) / m.linear.mae * 100)|round(0)|int }}% lower than the linear regression ({{ m.linear.mae }} ha). Tree-based models can capture interactions that a straight line cannot, for example how cattle and area reinforce each other.

The linear regression is still useful: on the logarithmic scale its R² is {{ m.linear.r2_log }}, and it is the easiest model to explain. Its weakness appears when the estimate is converted back to hectares, where a small log error becomes a large one for the biggest municipalities (RMSE {{ m.linear.rmse }} ha).
